# Seat objections to the operator's race mechanism — a SEPARATE proposal, not a merge

**Author:** Orchestrator seat (`claude-opus-5`), 2026-08-20. **Status:** proposal, for
Fable to adopt, reject, or replace with a third path.

**Why this is a separate file.** The seat first merged these objections into the
operator's own text. That was wrong: blending destroys the independence the exercise
exists to measure, and denies the adjudicator a clean choice. The operator: *"I would
have preferred your ideas stood on their own as they were and my ideas stood on their own
as they were and Fable could mix, match, or propose a third path."* So the operator's
mechanism stands as written in the seed brief; these three objections stand here.

**Standing caveat from the operator, which weighs against all three:** *"we are already
getting too deep into decide-by-committee. We need to learn from experience rather than
anticipate a clever tournament before trying."* Every objection below is an *anticipated*
problem. None has been observed. Fable should weigh that: a mitigation for a problem we
have not yet met is itself the complexity the operator is warning about, and the honest
answer may be "run it and see."

## Objection 1 — incoherence

A document assembled from the best paragraphs of seven may contradict itself. Sections
optimised separately do not necessarily compose: two excellent passages can rest on
incompatible assumptions, and the seams are where that shows.

*Proposed mitigation:* aspect selection, then a single **coherence pass owned by one
author**, whose job is internal consistency only, not further selection. Committee
chooses the parts; one hand makes them agree.

*Argument against:* this may be a phantom. If the aspects selected are mostly
self-contained (a test idea, a safety guard, a citation), composition may be trivial and
the extra pass is ceremony.

## Objection 2 — convergence to mush

If every output becomes an input to every model, round two may anchor on round one.
Diversity is the thing that made round one worth running; feeding everyone everything is
a mechanism for destroying it.

*Proposed mitigation:* cap the rounds, and **measure divergence between them** — treat
sharp convergence as a finding about the method rather than as success.

*Argument against:* convergence might be exactly right. If seven models converge on the
same spec after seeing each other's work, that could be genuine agreement on a good
answer rather than herding. The measurement is cheap and worth taking either way; the cap
is the contestable part.

## Objection 3 — premature lock-in

Narrowing a phase's roster on n=1 or n=2 observations is the folklore this project is
explicitly trying to replace ("race, don't decide"). Two data points on one task type is
not a skill assessment.

*Proposed mitigation:* a stated observation count before narrowing, plus periodic
re-test, since models change under the same label and the roster-epoch mechanism already
records that.

*Argument against:* perfect evidence is unaffordable. If a lane is plainly bad at a phase
after two attempts, spending four more to be rigorous about it is its own waste. The
operator's instinct — coarse grades, discovered dimensions, learn by doing — may simply
be the right cost/rigour trade.

## What the seat is NOT objecting to

Aspect-wise consolidation over winner-takes-all; all models including Fable authoring
independently, freshly, blind; a separate fresh Fable holding first and final word;
passes as a menu rather than a treadmill. The seat proposed some of these and the
operator improved the rest. No disagreement is recorded on any of them.
