## The single most important thing learned today

**Five separate guards misclassified by matching text a worker was merely reading or quoting.**

| guard | what it matched | cost |
|---|---|---|
| refusal classifier | a 429 quoted from a two-day-old document (T526) | a watchdog kill laundered as a provider refusal |
| refusal classifier | the bare word `quota` in prose (T601) | a successful run recorded as never reaching the model |
| directive gate | a row whose subject *was* the kill machinery (T643) | a zombie row; heal suppressed |

The correction is one architectural rule, and it is now ruling **§7c.31**: *a guard may stop a
lane, but no guard may attribute.* Classify from the **runner's own terminal record**
(`untracked/runs/T<id>.json`), never from a grep over worker stdout, which is untrusted for this
purpose. T625 and T630 applied it to the refusal classifier; T628 reintroduced the same bug in
the window watcher hours later. **Expect to find more.** T652's harness-symmetry audit exists to
enumerate them rather than wait for the next incident.

Second-order: **that guard has never once refused correctly.** Every observed firing was a false
positive, and it has no override — so this seat routed around it via `bin/subagent`, which has no
guard at all. A guard people route around teaches everyone to route around guards. T677 owns it.
