### The three regimes (the territory/loop thread, resolved)

The operator's hypothesis was that indefinite loops are mutual territory-denial. It resolved into
three regimes, and **the middle one falsifies the hypothesis**:

1. **3×3** — someone can force pass-alive life; value single and rule-independent; the winner annihilates.
2. **4×3 / 4×4** — **nobody** can force pass-alive life, yet Black wins decisively on area. *This is
   the operator's loss.* It cuts toward: **immortality is the wrong notion of territory for decisive wins.**
3. **2×2 / 3×2** — mutual denial; draw-by-loop roots.

### Two laws that survived

- **Root `L == H` ⟺ some player can force Benson life** — holds at **5/5 sizes**. Read carefully:
  forcible immortality buys **rule-independence**, not victory.
- **Any state containing a Benson-alive chain is `L == H`** — **99,133,036 entries, zero exceptions**,
  and **unexplained**. It is *not* score-forced: alive-and-**losing** states exist (lowest `L==H==−2`
  with a two-stone alive chain). This is the strongest unexplained regularity we have.

### Settled directions — do not re-derive these

- **Draw-by-loop ⟹ neither player can force life** (confinement). Exact through 3×3; 32 exceptions at
  4×3; **9,376 of 895,216** at 4×4.
- **The converse is FALSE.** 85.3% of the neither-can-force class at 4×4 is **decisive**
  (19,518,338 of 22,885,430). `canForceLife = false` for both is **not** a draw certificate. Two agents
  have already been caught re-deriving this; a third will try.
- **SCC membership is near-vacuous** as a bracket predictor (100% of `L<H` in a non-trivial SCC — but so
  is 98.1% of `L==H`). **Ko-freeness points the wrong way** (93.8% of bracketed 3×3 entries are ko-free).

### Territory census

- Coexisting unconditional life begins at **4×3** (2 / 321,689) and is **324 / 24,318,165** at 4×4.
- At 3×3, unconditional life **without a centre stone** exists: 206 / 1,766 alive positions, **38**
  shapes mod D4. *The operator's shape claim was refuted.*

### The capture budget — measured, and demoted

| property | reading |
|---|---|
| termination | 0 back edges at 3×3 and 4×3 (`B=8`); control fires at 135,512 / 4,929,916 — **holds** |
| Bellman consistency | 0 mismatches / 793,619 states — **holds** |
| **agreement with the true value** | 3×3 root converges to `+9`; **4×3 root oscillates forever in {0,1,2,7,9,12} and never reaches the true +4** — **FALSE** |

**Ruling (ADR-0022): it is a different game, not an approximation of ours.** Sound as a *terminating*
construction, wrong as a *valuing* one; it may never write to the canonical table. The lesson
generalises: **termination and Bellman consistency were both true and neither implies correctness —
internal consistency is not external agreement.**

## 2. What we KNOW WE DON'T KNOW

1. **Why is `life ⟹ L==H` true?** Categorical over 99.1M entries, zero exceptions, no proof, and not
   score-forced. **The best theorem candidate in the project.**
2. **Is optimal play ever *forced* into a loop?** — the operator's sibling test: is a loopy child ever
   the *only* optimal child? **In flight (T412).** If the count is zero, truncation costs nothing and
   the loop worry dissolves. If not, the ply cap is concealing something real.
3. **What is the honest ply cap per size?** The tables carry `dtt`; nobody has read out `max dtt` over
   terminating entries. **In flight (T412).** If 3×3's true figure is ≈27 and T407 capped 96% of games
   at 400, then those games capped for some *other* reason and finding it is the result.
4. **Can looping be predicted cheaply?** `L<H` marks it exactly but is the answer, not a predictor.
   Three candidate features are already refuted (above). **Open.**
5. **What is forcible at exactly the decisive roots?** `canForceLife` is too strong (4×3/4×4 are
   decisive without it); a score floor is trivial. Something sits between — Fable's
   "can-force-settled-and-ahead" is the candidate. **This formalises the operator's "strong territory".**
6. **Is the 4×3 root `[+4,+12]` right?** Measured by **one instrument, never audited.** It gates
   anything downstream that cites it.
7. **What does anything cost?** Two implementations agreeing on every result may differ by an order of
   magnitude in resources. **Nothing measures this today.**
8. **Track A** — regenerate the `KO_SENSITIVE` columns with `memo_writes=false`. Until then those values
   are distrusted and every 4×4 headline carries that caveat.

## 3. Where our DOUBTS are

- **Tooling, not the kernel.** Every defect of this week was in tooling or an instrument; none in
  `rules.zig`/`retro.zig`/the tables. **The shared failure mode: the tool reported success while doing
  nothing** — a `u6` loop that ran 14h looking busy; `managent tell` reporting a directive it had made
  unreadable; `absorb` and `gen-indices` reading a 221-row register as *empty*; an agent replying
  `OK.` and executing nothing. **A silent wrong answer outranks a loud crash.**
- **Claims outrunning evidence — including mine.** I asserted the Ollama five-agent cap was falsified
  by a probe that finished in nine seconds on warm sessions and **could not have detected it**.
  Standing wording: *the cap exists; absence of an observed effect is not evidence of absence.* This is
  now audit-gate check 7 on every sprint.
- **Everything unproven is a plugin.** A finite tournament can only *fail to refute* — it can never
  establish "strictly better". Only a proof promotes a change from plugin to correct.
- **The 3×3 leg of the tournament is uninformative**, not reassuring: 96% capped, and its starts were
  bracketed positions where cycling is expected by construction.
