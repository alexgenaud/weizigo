**Author:** Opus 5, at the user's request ("critique the facts, mistakes,
motivations, what's possible, what's impractical, what should be done now, what
might be done later"). Companion documents: `CLAIMS.md` (the claim register with

## 0. The goal, restated

The user's goal, stated 2026-07-28, supersedes the working goal the project has
been optimising:

> Play the optimal game of Go. **Know when the play is optimal and when it is
> not.** Shrink the suboptimal play toward zero. If PSK is impossible, relax or
> find alternatives. Play optimal Go under **standard rules**.

Three things follow immediately, and the project's current plan satisfies none
of them.

1. **The deliverable is a player, not a table.** The table is a means. The
   project has been optimising the means for months and the player has received
   comparatively little scrutiny — until 2026-07-27 nobody had checked whether
   its move rule was even well-defined. It was not.
2. **"Know when the play is optimal" is a *certification* requirement.** It is
   a first-class deliverable, not a nicety, and it is *cheaper* than optimality.
   An engine that plays adequately and knows exactly when it is guessing is more
   useful — and more honest — than one that plays well and cannot tell.
3. **"Standard rules" is not one rule**, and the project has never picked one.
   See §3.

## 2. The central mistake — and it is a framing mistake, not a coding one

**JUDGEMENT.** PSK is **non-Markovian**: legality depends on unbounded history.
A position→score table is **Markovian**. The project built the second for the
first. Every crisis is a symptom of that one mismatch:

- C2 falsified — because a single score cannot encode history-dependence.
- C3 falsified — because a bracket over a history-free fixpoint does not bound
  a history-dependent value.
- C4 false — restatement of the same thing.
- The `ko_ref >= d` bug (ADR-0013) — an attempt to *cache* across histories,
  i.e. to pretend the problem away, which failed exactly where predicted.
- The 2026-07-27 chainability collapse — the player consuming the mismatch.

**The pattern worth naming:** at each falsification the project weakened the
**claim** rather than fixing the **representation**. Single score → bracket →
"fresh-start-only oracle". Three retreats, each honest, none addressing the
cause. The fresh-start-only reframe (B05/B11) is the current position, and it is
a description of what the artifact *is*, not a path to what the user *wants*.

**In fairness** — and this matters, because the retreats were not foolish — the
project's central hypothesis was reasonable and it *ran the right experiment*:
if most positions were history-insensitive, a Markovian table plus a small
bracketed remainder would have worked. T13 killed it at the smallest goban that
could express it. That is good science. The criticism is of what happened
*next*: the falsification should have triggered a representation review, and
instead triggered a scope reduction.

## 3. The rules problem — the project has been validating against the wrong game

**FACT**, already in the repo but not reflected in the headline claims
(`../research/retrograde-3x3.md:224-245`): MIGOS II — the source of every
published anchor this project cites — plays **basic ko + long-cycle ties**, not
superko. weizigo plays **PSK**. These are *different games*. They agree at 3×3
(+9) and 4×4 (+2) and **provably disagree at 2×2 and 2×3** (published 0/0 vs
weizigo's exhaustively ground-truthed +1/+1).

So "4×4 = +2 matches the published anchor" — cited in `PROGRESS.md` as a
validation — is agreement between two different games on a goban where the
difference happens not to bite. That is *weak* evidence of correctness, and the
project has been treating it as strong.

**The same pattern, quantified (added 2026-07-28).** `ruleset-options.md:205-215`
tabulates the empty-goban brackets against the published anchors and captions it
**"Soundness confirmed: every known anchor score falls inside its bracket."**
Measure how much that confirms:

| goban | bracket | width | plausible score range | anchor | P(a random score lands in-bracket) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3×3 | [2, 9] | 7 | ~19 values | **+9** — *the bracket's own upper endpoint* | ~42% |
| 4×3 | [−1, 12] | 13 | ~25 values | +4 | ~56% |
| 4×4 | [−6, 16] | 22 | ~33 values | +2 | ~70% |

Each "✓ in-bracket" therefore carries on the order of **one bit** of evidence,
and the 4×4 row barely half of one. A test a wrong answer passes 70% of the
time is close to unfalsifiable. This is the third instance of the same
methodological fault in this section — anchor agreement, cycle-rule
insensitivity, and now bracket containment, all weak evidence recorded as
strong. **JUDGEMENT:** the project should require every validation to state the
probability that a *wrong* result would also have passed it. That single
question would have caught all three.

**On "standard rules".** There is no single standard, and the differences are
exactly in the area that is hard:

| Ruleset | Repetition rule | Tractable here? |
|---|---|---|
| Tromp–Taylor (the machine/machine standard; KataGo, Leela) | **positional superko** | **No** — this is the intractable one |
| AGA / Chinese (modern codifications) | situational or positional superko | No |
| Japanese | basic ko + **no result** on long cycles | Plausibly |
| MIGOS II (the anchors) | basic ko + long-cycle **tie** | Plausibly |

**This is the uncomfortable finding for the stated goal:** the machine-to-machine
standard *is* PSK, and PSK is the rule proven intractable. There is no version of
this project that exactly solves the machine standard on any interesting goban.
The goal must therefore be approached from below, with a **measured** gap. §5 is
how.

## 4. Method faults — four, in order of cost

**M-F1. Claims have no dependency edges.** When C2 fell, nothing mechanically
determined what else fell. This is the fault the user identified unprompted
("what knowledge is dependent upon prior knowledge") and it is the most
expensive one, because it makes every falsification a manual re-audit. Being
fixed now in `CLAIMS.md`, which must distinguish `derives-from` (parent falls →
child falls) from `evidenced-by` (parent falls → child becomes untested).

**M-F2. "Measured on artifact X" is conflated with "true of the algorithm".**
M-facts describe a specific `.wzo` file; FP-claims describe the method. This is
how "the committed ko-sensitive values are untrustworthy" coexists in the same
tree with "4×4 is solved". The register must carry the distinction in the scope
column.

**M-F5. Load-bearing evidence lives in git-ignored scratch, and has been
deleted.** Found by `CLAIMS.md` §7 while sweeping, outside its brief, and
verified: `untracked/` is git-ignored (`.gitignore:8`), and the primary evidence
for **T13** (the C2 falsification — the falsification the entire current
strategy rests on), **T02/B1** (which removed the re-converge check from FP1
acceptance), **T07** (the audit that rewrote the whole 4×4 tree) and **B05**
(the reframe scope) is **gone from disk**. T13's numbers survive in git at
`docs/research/c2-falsification-3x2.md`, which is why it is still marked PROVEN
— but its probe source does not, so the reproduction block in that very file
cannot be executed. **B1 has no durable evidence file at all.**

The mechanism is already documented in `arena-4x4-undef.md:10-18`, where a
cleanup bundle deleted a concurrent bundle's write target — the project noticed
the failure mode once, drew the right lesson ("write durable findings to git
`docs/research/` directly"), and did not apply it retroactively. **QA-022.**

**Judgement:** this is more serious than any single technical bug in this
review. A claim marked PROVEN whose evidence cannot be retrieved is not proven;
it is remembered. The project's epistemic tree is, in several load-bearing
places, a set of citations to files that no longer exist.

**M-F3. Auditors ship without calibration.** I made this error myself on
2026-07-27: I read the chainability violations as the `ko_ref` bug until I ran
the same sweep on the *known-good* 2×2/3×2 artifacts and found identical
behaviour. An auditor with no passing case cannot distinguish "bug" from
"definition" — every input looks guilty. **The #2 self-consistency auditor,
which AGENTS.md makes a mandatory pre-commit gate, has no documented calibration
case.** That should be fixed before it gates anything else.

**M-F0. The deepest orphan: every shipped ko-sensitive value rests on a
falsified claim — and Track A does not fix it.** Promoted to the top of this
list on 2026-07-28, after `CLAIMS.md` found it (O1) and I verified the
escalation from source.

    GLOBAL.F2 (bracket-guided finisher sound) — CLAIMED
      derives-from  ADR-0010: brackets "hold under ANY arrival history" (0010:16-18)
        derives-from  GLOBAL.C3  —  FALSE-AS-SCOPED at 3×3 (E2)

The project had already written that edge — `open-hypotheses-2026-07-27.md`
says outright that cutting on `[L,H]` under a real history **is** C3 — but
applied it only to a hypothetical *play-time* search. Never to the finisher
that generated **every shipped ko-sensitive value**. `src/retro.zig:464` states
the claim in a code comment: *"bracket cut: [lo,hi] holds under ANY arrival
history -> KO_CLEAN"*.

**My escalation, verified from source and not in `CLAIMS.md`:** `bracketed` and
`memo_writes` are **independent** parameters (`Retro.finishProgress`,
`Finisher.init`), and `saveArtifact` hardcodes `bracketed = true`
(`src/retro.zig:2407`). So **Track A does not escape this.** ADR-0013 Track A
turns off the GHI memo (`memo_writes=false`); it leaves bracket cuts on. Every
artifact this project has ever produced — writes-on, writes-off, and any future
Track A regen — rests on the bracket claim. The 1.67% writes-off "definitional
floor" I measured on 2026-07-27 is *also* bracket-derived.

**Nuance that must survive, because it is the whole question.** E2 falsified C3
for *real-game* arrival histories. The finisher's cuts fire under *search-path*
histories descending from a fresh-start root — a different family. Whether they
are the same claim is unresolved. **But ADR-0010 asserts they are** ("ANY
arrival history"), so either ADR-0010's own justification is too strong, or F2
is orphaned. There is no third option, and the project should pick one in an
ADR rather than leave it implicit. **QA-018 / QA-019.**

**M-F4. Per-goban independence is applied selectively.** AGENTS.md forbids
inheriting a claim across sizes. But `boards/4x4/EPISTEMIC.md` F1 inherits the
`ko_ref` bug from 3×2 to 4×4 on the grounds that "the guard is the same code."
That reasoning is probably *right* — a claim about code is not a claim about a
goban — but the rule as written forbids it, so the project is either violating
its own rule or the rule is mis-stated. **My judgement: the rule is mis-stated.**
It should distinguish *empirical* claims about a goban (never inherit) from
*structural* claims about code or mathematics (inherit, with the argument
written down). The inheritance audit in `CLAIMS.md` will enumerate the cases.

*(Note: the 2026-07-27 writes-off comparison — 1.67% vs 4.08% at 4×4 — is the
first 4×4-**specific** evidence for F1, which until now rested entirely on the
3×2 measurement plus that inheritance argument.)*

## 5. The constructive proposal — a rule ladder with a measurable gap

**JUDGEMENT, offered as the main recommendation. UNTESTED.**

The user's instinct — "if 3-ko, 2-ko, or basic 1-ko is all that we can handle,
then we will solve that" — is not a concession. It is the right structure, and
it can be made principled.

**Make the rule's history-dependence an explicit, bounded part of the state, and
treat its size as a dial.**

Two readings of the dial, both worth costing (they are not the same and the
project should choose deliberately):

- **Reading A — history window `k`.** Illegal to recreate any position from the
  last `k` plies. State: `(position, previous k−1 positions)`. `k=1` ≈ basic ko;
  `k→∞` = PSK.
- **Reading B — simultaneous ko points `j`.** State: `(position, side, set of
  ≤ j currently-forbidden points)`. `j=1` = simple ko. This is what Go
  *terminology* means by double/triple ko, and the project already uses
  "2-ko+" language for the 83K unfilled 4×4 slots — so Reading B is probably the
  one that matches existing tooling.

**Every setting of the dial is Markovian by construction.** Therefore the table
is **chainable by construction**, the existing retrograde machinery applies
unchanged, and C2/C3/C4 do not arise — not because they were solved, but because
the representation no longer provokes them. The GHI problem, the `ko_ref` guard,
the bracket-soundness question: all of it is an artifact of trying to be
Markovian about a non-Markovian rule.

### Why this serves the goal specifically

1. **"Optimal" becomes a claim we can actually make.** Under rule R_j the
   engine is exactly optimal, with no certificate needed. Today the engine is
   provably optimal under **no** stated rule — not even fresh-start PSK, per the
   ply-14 evidence.
2. **"Know when it is not optimal" becomes measurable.** Solve at `j` and `j+1`;
   compare on reachable states. Agreement is evidence of convergence (not proof
   — `j+2` could differ, and that must be stated every time). Disagreement
   *localises exactly which positions need deeper history*. That set, and its
   measure over reachable play, **is** the suboptimality the user wants driven
   toward zero.
3. **It degrades honestly.** If `j=1` is all that fits, we ship a player that is
   perfect under simple ko and has a *measured* gap to the next rung — rather
   than a player that is perfect under nothing and does not know it.

### What must be checked before any of this is believed

- **Cost.** `j=1` census first — the reachable `(position, ko_point, side)`
  count at 4×4 (H1 in `../research/open-hypotheses-2026-07-27.md`). `j=2` is
  likely much worse; Reading A's `k=2` needs the *edge* count of the position
  graph, plausibly ~16× positions, and `k=3` almost certainly does not fit.
  **The ladder may have only two usable rungs.** That is still better than zero.
- **Correctness of the rung.** A free falsification target already exists: a
  correct simple-ko build must reproduce the published **0** at 2×2 and 2×3,
  **not** weizigo's PSK +1. Seconds to check, on the smallest gobans, before any
  4×4 effort.
- **Long cycles.** Simple ko alone does not terminate them. "Long cycle = tie"
  (the MIGOS rule) is *plausibly* a loopy-game fixpoint — L/H iteration with
  loops pinned to the tie value — but this is **UNVERIFIED** and is the single
  biggest open design risk in the proposal. `score-on-cycle` is foreclosed as
  being as hard as PSK, and any long-cycle rule must not smuggle it back in.

## 6. What should be done now, and what later

Ordered by information-per-unit-cost, not by appeal.

### Now — cheap, and each one changes a decision

1. **The `j=1` reachable-state census at 4×4.** Counting, not solving. The only
   experiment that can *retire the representation* rather than raise confidence
   in it. A NO-GO is equally informative: it closes the last named tractable
   generation candidate.
2. **The certified-fraction baseline.** What share of the moves it actually
   plays can the engine certify? Slot-uniform ko-sensitivity is 21.27% at 4×4,
   but 16 of 19 plies in both regression games are flagged. If the reachable
   figure is near 84%, the fresh-start table is nearly worthless *as a player*
   even though it is 78.68% single-score *as a table*. This is the user's
   headline metric and it has never been measured. **In flight.**
3. **`CLAIMS.md` with dependency edges.** Cheap, mechanical, and it is what
   makes every future falsification cost minutes instead of a session.
   **In flight.**
4. **Exhaustive 4×4 chainability** — one command, removes the 1:37 sampling
   caveat from the headline 2026-07-27 result.
5. **Calibrate the #2 auditor** (M-F3). It gates commits; it should have a
   documented known-good and known-bad case.

### Later — real work, only after the above

6. Simple-ko generation, if the census says GO; validated against the 2×2/2×3
   falsification target *first*.
7. The long-cycle resolution rule — needs an ADR and probably a small proof.
8. Player hardening. Note the sharper variant found on 2026-07-27: rather than
   muting the player on the whole ko-sensitive region, **verify the Bellman
   identity at the current node before trusting the comparison** (~16 table
   lookups). The empty 4×4 goban is KO_SENSITIVE yet the identity *holds* there
   and through ply 7 — flagged means *unguaranteed*, not *wrong*, and only 4.08%
   of ko-sensitive slots actually violate. A per-node check would keep those
   plies and refuse exactly at ply 8. **UNTESTED**; strength cost unmeasured.
9. 5×5. **Judgement: not now, and not for a long time.** It is the natural pull
   of the project and it is the wrong move while the engine is optimal under no
   stated rule on a goban 1/25th the size.

### Explicitly de-prioritised

- Further characterisation of C2/C3 leak *magnitudes* at larger sizes. Both are
  falsified; measuring how badly a dead claim fails does not advance the goal.
- 4×4 C2-probe (T19) and 4×4 E2. Both are characterisation, and both epistemic
  trees already say so.

## 8. The one-line summary

The engine is currently **optimal under no stated rule**, on any goban. The
fastest route to the user's goal is not a bigger table — it is to pick a rule
the state can actually represent, be exactly optimal under it, and then measure
the gap to the standard rule instead of hoping it is zero.
