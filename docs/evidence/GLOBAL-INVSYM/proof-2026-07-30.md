## I. Colour inversion: `L(−pos,−side) == −H(pos,side)`

**Definition.** The negation operator `ν : 𝕃 → 𝕃`,
`(νx)(i, s, p) := −x(neg(i), −s, p)`. Since `neg` permutes cells and negates
values: `ν(⊤) = ⊥`, `ν(⊥) = ⊤`.

**Lemma (Bellman commutation).** `T ∘ ν = ν ∘ T`. *Proof, one cell `(i,s,p)`:*
negate the update at `(neg(i), −s, p)`. Its move-point set equals that of
`(i, s, p)` (eye-prune included: `benson_alive`/`is_own_eye` are colour-blind);
its child set is exactly `{neg(ci)}` with side flipped to `s` (fact 2), reading
`x(neg(ci), s, 0) = −(νx)(ci, −s, 0)`; its pass terms read `score(neg(i)) =
−score_i` (fact 3) and `x(neg(i), s, 1) = −(νx)(i, −s, 1)`. Every read of `x` at
the negated cell is the negation of a read of `νx` at the original cell. Now the
one line of algebra that does the work — since `opt_{−s}(−a₁,…,−aₖ) =
−opt_s(a₁,…,aₖ)` (max of negated = − of min, and dually):

    −T(x)(neg i, −s, p) = −opt_{−s}( −terms ) = opt_s( terms ) = T(νx)(i, s, p).

Settled cells: pinned at `score(neg i) = −score_i`, so pinning commutes with `ν`.
Kill-X% edges: the predicate is a sign-symmetric opponent-stone count (fires on
exactly corresponding children) and its replacement value is `area_score`, odd —
commutes as well. ∎

**Theorem (inversion half).** By induction on the seed iterates (GLOBAL.FP1):
`ν(T^k(⊤)) = T^k(ν⊤) = T^k(⊥)`; both orbits stabilise in finitely many sweeps
(finite lattice height), giving `ν(H) = L`, i.e. componentwise

    L(−pos, −side, p) = −H(pos, side, p)   and dually   H(−pos, −side, p) = −L(pos, side, p).

Negating colours swaps max/min roles, hence **swaps the fixpoints** (`0009:102-107`) —
the exhaustive inversion check must pair L with H, not L with itself. ∎

**Corollaries.** (1) On the fresh-start single-score region `L == H =: V`, the
plain ADR-0008 identity: `V(−pos,−side) = −V(pos,side)`. (2) Any *unique*
fixpoint `F` of `T` is self-dual, `ν(F) = F` (since `ν(F)` is again a fixpoint:
`T νF = ν T F = νF`). (3) Sweep scheduling is immaterial: the computed tables are
the schedule-invariant lfp/gfp (GLOBAL.FP1, Bertsekas hand-off), and `ν` is a
cell permutation composed with negation, so the identity holds of the tables the
engine writes, not merely of an idealised synchronous iterate.

## II. Dihedral symmetry: transforms never change score or sign

**Group.** The goban's grid-isometry group: full `D₄` (8 elements) for square
gobans (2×2, 3×3, 4×4); the Klein group `V₄` (4 elements — identity, 180°
rotation, two flips) for rectangular gobans (3×2, 4×3), since 90° rotation maps
`w×h` to the different object `h×w`. ADR-0008's "all 8 dihedral transforms"
(`0008:48`) is the square case of the same statement.

**Action and commutation.** For `g` in the group, `ĝ` permutes positions
pointwise; define `(g·x)(i, s, p) := x(ĝ⁻¹(i), s, p)` — side and value sign
untouched. Fact 2 under isometries: children of `ĝ(i)` are exactly `{ĝ(ci)}`;
fact 3: `score(ĝ(i)) = score_i`; `opt_s` is unchanged (side unchanged); settled
pinning is invariant. Hence `T(g·x) = g·T(x)` — the same one-cell algebra with
no sign flips. The seeds are constant vectors, `g·(±N) = ±N`, so induction and
finite stabilisation give `g·L = L` and `g·H = H` **separately**:

    L(g·pos, side) = L(pos, side),   H(g·pos, side) = H(pos, side)  —

dihedral transforms stay **within** each table (`0009:106`) and never change
score or sign (`0008:60`). ∎
