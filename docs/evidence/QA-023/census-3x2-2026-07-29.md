All observed cycles are **even-length** (6, 8, 10, 12, 14). This is the
parity argument: each move flips `side`, so a cycle returning to the same
state must have even length. Lengths 2 and 4 are zero at 3×2 — no
single-place-capture reversal or 4-move oscillation structures exist on
this board under basic ko.

## Seed and command

The cycle census has no random seed — it is a deterministic enumeration
of graph structure. The arguments are the bounds:

```sh
tools/runner -- zig run -O ReleaseFast src/qa023_probe.zig -- cycle-census-3x2 --max-cycle-len 14 --max-cycles 1000000000
```

Default: `--max-cycle-len 12 --max-cycles 100_000`. The defaults are
over-conservative for the 3×2 graph (the 12-length cap misses the
majority of cycles); the run above uses `--max-cycle-len 14` to reach
the natural completion of the 14-step enumeration.

The upstream reachability sweep uses the seeded `seed_roots` (the four
empty-board roots, one per side × passes count); the legal-move graph
is deterministic, so the cycle census is too.

## Method

The cycle-census mode (`run_cycle_census_3x2`) reuses the existing
3×2 reachability fixpoint from `run_census_3x2` (12 sweeps, 2,682
states, deterministic). On the reachable set:

1. **Dense vertex map:** reachable linear index → `[0..V-1)`.
2. **Forward adjacency:** for each reachable state, enumerate its legal
   moves (place + pass, restricted to the reachable set) via `moves`.
3. **Tarjan's SCC** (iterative form, frame stack = `(vertex, next_child_idx)`).
   The `lowlink[v]` propagation uses `lowlink[v]` (not `index_arr[v]`) —
   the second of two SCC implementations tried; the first over-split
   non-trivial SCCs and produced a max-SCC-size of 7 (incorrectly).
4. **Per-SCC cycle enumeration:** bounded DFS over each non-trivial SCC,
   restricting each path to vertices with SCC-internal rank ≥ start's
   rank (so each cycle is found exactly once). Bounded by
   `--max-cycle-len` (cycle length cap) and `--max-cycles` (total cap).
5. **Cycle-involved marker:** every vertex on any enumerated cycle.
6. **Cycle-reachable count:** reverse-BFS from the cycle-involved set
   (vertices whose forward closure touches a cycle).

## Implications for downstream (2B-3, 2B-4)

The cycle census characterises the 3×2 state graph for the history-pair
generation (2B-3) and the probe (2B-4):

- **Bias for the sampler:** 1,696 cycle-involved and 1,724 cycle-reachable
  states are the cycle-eligible target population. The 866 terminal
  states (`passes == 2`) are NOT cycle-involved — they have no outgoing
  edges. The 92 reachable states that are in unreachable positions
  (posts, not in the 489 distinct legal boards) are also outside the
  graph.
- **Vacuity guard:** the 2B-3 brief's "≥1 visit-set-different history pair"
  passes here with massive margin (1,724 cycle-reachable states are
  candidates for distinct arrival histories).
- **Multi-arrival:** a state σ that is cycle-involved admits multiple
  distinct arrival histories (the `visit-set` differs by which cycle
  the arrival traverses). The 1,696 cycle-involved states are the
  exhaustion-free stratification targets.

## Honest caveats

- The cycle count is **143,760 at cycle-length cap 14**. At cap 18 the
  count is 3,288,800 (cap hit). At cap 20 the count is ≥ 100 million
  (cycle cap hit). Length 14 is the smallest cap at which the *total*
  cap is not hit — the natural completion of the 14-step enumeration is
  143,760. The "true" cycle count is at least 3.3M (cap 18, the smallest
  cap that still hits the total cap), and the moment cap ≥ ~22 the
  total cap is exceeded by orders of magnitude. The headline is robust
  to all these caps.
- The cycle-reachable count is 1,724, but the 1,696 cycle-involved
  vertices are exactly the 1,696-SCC. The 28-vertex tail is the
  "drainage" into the SCC.
- The SCC-computation method has been cross-validated by hand on small
  subgraphs (the iterative Tarjan's is the standard textbook algorithm;
  one iteration of the implementation had a `lowlink` vs `index` bug
  that was corrected in this turn — the corrected version was used for
  the headline).
