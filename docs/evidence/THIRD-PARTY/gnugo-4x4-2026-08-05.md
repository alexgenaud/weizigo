# T381 — GNU Go, Pachi, Fuego against the 4×4 oracle: an opponent with no code, no table, no author in common

Task: T381 · Role: worker · Model: deepseek-v4-flash · Date: 2026-08-05
Identifier: `flash/T381` (abbr. `flash/T381`)

**Landmark line: L2 (proven 4×4 values) — advanced.** For the first time the
4×4 oracle was played against engines that share no code, no table and no
author with it: GNU Go 3.8, Pachi 12.90 and Fuego 1.1.SVN, driven over GTP
by an independent instrument (`src/t381_evse.zig`). On the T366 corpus (33
openings) plus the empty goban, both colours — 68 games per engine, 204
games total — the oracle lost **zero** games (and tied zero games) from a
position its own table claimed won for the side we were playing:
**losses from claimed-won positions = 0 / 68 per engine**. Every one of the
engines' combined 55 losses came from openings the table already called
lost for that colour (the corpus's forced openings are often losing), and
every claimed-won game was won. One genuine ruleset discovery was made and
fixed on the way: **Fuego's default is positional superko, not basic ko** —
its first run produced the only tie-from-claimed finding of the project,
resolved to cause 1 (ruleset mismatch, a different game) and eliminated by
`go_param_rules ko_rule simple`, after which the same corpus ran clean.

---

## 1. Why this row exists — strength is irrelevant, independence is everything

Every check before this compared two of *our own* artifacts (old-vs-new,
L3) or our engine against itself. A third-party engine cannot be wrong in
the same way we are. The test is asymmetric:

> If our table says a position is won for the side we are playing, and we
> then lose it to any opponent, one of exactly three things is true: the
> ruleset was mismatched, our move selector is wrong, or the table is
> wrong. All three are findings.

Our engine winning proves nothing and losing a table-lost position proves
nothing. **Only a loss from a claimed-won position is informative** — and
every one must be resolved to a cause, never averaged into a score.

## 2. Ruleset matching comes first — and it found a real mismatch

The MIGOS epitaph applies: a different cycle-resolution rule is a different
game. Before any game counts, the opponent's actual rules were established
and recorded, against **our ruleset: Chinese area scoring, komi 0, no
suicide, basic ko** (rules_id 3, `data/oracle-4x4-v2.wzo2`).

### 2.1 The probes

`weizigo-t381-evse --probe --engine <name>` runs a fixed probe sequence:
ko (a genuine simple-ko shape — single Black stone at B3 with its only
liberty at B2, its other three neighbours White, B2's other neighbours
Black; after `play w B2` captures B3 the recapture `play b B3` must be
rejected), suicide (`play b D4` in a shape where D4 has no liberties must
be rejected), and `final_score` sanity on an empty and a full board.

| probe | GNU Go 3.8 | Pachi 12.90 | Fuego 1.1.SVN | our rules require |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ko recapture `play b B3` after W captures at B2 | rejected (`illegal move`) | rejected (`illegal move`) | rejected (`illegal move: … B3 (simple)`) | rejected |
| suicide `play b D4` (no liberties, no capture) | rejected (`illegal move`) | rejected (`illegal move`) | rejected (`illegal move: … D4 (suicide)`) | rejected |
| `final_score` on empty board (komi 0) | **B+16.0** (wrong; 0 expected) | 0 ✓ | 0 ✓ | 0 |
| `final_score` on full 8-8 board | **B+16.0** (wrong; 0 expected) | **`unclear groups`** | W+16 (wrong; 0 expected — Fuego's own dead-stone judgment) | 0 |

**Finding (recorded, ruleset-adjacent): every engine's `final_score` is
unreliable on 4×4.** GNU Go 3.8 returns B+16.0 for an empty board and for a
full 8-8 board; Pachi returns `unclear groups` on a full board; Fuego
returns W+16 for an 8-8 position (it applies its own dead-stone judgment,
which is not pure area scoring). **The game result is therefore never taken
from the engine.** The final position is the shared truth (both sides'
moves are validated under our rules), and the score is our area score of
that position — the pure-geometric Chinese area count (stones + surrounded
empty points, no dead-stone removal) that the table's own terminal values
use. Engines' `final_score` is recorded per game as data and never used.

### 2.2 The Fuego superko discovery — a ruleset mismatch found and fixed

Fuego's default repetition rule is **positional superko**, not basic ko
(its `go_param_rules` reports `ko_rule superko`). In the first Fuego run
(68 games under default rules) this surfaced exactly where the brief
predicts: **fuego-o13-B** (opening random-010-cabd, we are Black, root
claimed [16,16]) reached ply 48 with us playing a table-optimal move every
time, and Fuego **rejected our 49th move** — a position-repeating `A4`,
legal under basic ko, illegal under superko:

```
? illegal move: 49 B A4 (superko)
```

The game truncated at that point (tie 0 recorded), producing the run's only
finding: `tie_from_claimed`, resolved to **cause 1 — ruleset mismatch**
(the two engines were playing different games, exactly the MIGOS trap). The
same 48-ply sequence replayed into a fresh Fuego under default rules
reproduces the rejection; replayed with `go_param_rules ko_rule simple`
(the same sequence, same coordinates) it is **accepted with zero errors** —
Fuego *can* be configured to basic ko. With that setting the full corpus
re-ran clean: **0 findings, 0 ruleset events, fuego-o13-B wins B+16.**

> The default-rules Fuego run and its one tie-from-claimed finding are
> preserved in the run log and context JSON. The published Fuego numbers
> are the basic-ko run. An engine that *cannot* be configured to our
> ruleset is a clean exclusion per the brief; Fuego could, so it plays.

### 2.3 Invariants maintained during play

- Every engine move is validated by our rules (`pos_from_move` + basic-ko
  point + suicide rejection); an illegal move aborts the game and is
  recorded (`engine_illegal`). **Zero such events in 204 games.**
- Every move we send to the engine is a `play`; an engine rejection of our
  play is recorded (`engine_rejected`). **Zero in the published runs**
  (two in the default-superko Fuego run, both the same superko cause).
- Positional agreement is cross-checked at game end via `list_stones`
  (GNU Go, Fuego): **all 136 such checks agree**. Pachi lacks
  `list_stones`; for it the positional agreement is by construction (both
  sides apply the same validated move sequence) and is marked untested.
- Repetition handling in play: basic ko allows whole-board position
  repetition (a different game from PSK). One Fuego game
  (fuego-o32-W, basic-ko run) cycled to the 400-ply cap — a legal
  basic-ko cycle, reported as capped (1/68), not classified (T366
  convention), not a finding.

## 3. Method

**Instrument** — a new standalone tool, `src/t381_evse.zig` (SHA
`3e6426bd…`), compiled ad hoc with `zig build-exe` at HEAD `f46ba1ca`
(build.zig untouched; engine sources untouched; reads the artifact and the
committed session/table code `gtp.zig`/`rules.zig`/`artifact2.zig` only).

```
zig build-exe -O ReleaseFast --dep version -Mroot=src/t381_evse.zig \
  -Mversion=src/version.zig --cache-dir /tmp/weizigo/t381/cache \
  --global-cache-dir /tmp/weizigo/t381/global --name weizigo-t381-evse \
  -femit-bin=/tmp/weizigo/t381/t381-evse
```

**In-process play for our side** (the T366 caveat): `bin/weizigo-gtp`'s
GTP `play` enforces positional superko unconditionally, which would forbid
basic-ko lines containing position repetitions, so our side plays through
`gtp.Session` directly under `.basic_ko` enforcement — the same session
code the GTP consumer uses, with `choose_with_check` (the production move
selector, H5(a) chainability). Only the *opponent* is a GTP subprocess.

**Openings** — the T366 corpus verbatim (3 canonical one-ply + 30 seeded
random two-ply, seed 42, deduplicated = 33 distinct; verified
name-for-name against `findings/T366-engine-kifu.json`) **plus the empty
goban** = 34 openings × 2 colour assignments = 68 games per engine. Opening
plies are forced for both sides; play then continues with our side moving
from the table and the engine from `genmove`.

**Per game recorded:** final area score; our table's [L,H] bracket at every
position we moved from (with pinned value, claimed-for-us, ko-pending,
passes, ko-sensitive flag, in-scope); the **root bracket** (the first
position where we freely moved) and whether the outcome landed inside it;
the empty-goban start bracket for our colour; whether we won/tied/lost;
engine resignation / rejection / illegal-move events; the engine's own
`final_score` (as data).

**Claim semantics** (scores are always Black-positive; side-to-move picks
the array, never the sign): a position is claimed-won for us iff Black and
`L > 0`, or White and `H < 0` (equivalently `pinned ≠ 0` with the sign for
our colour). The root claim is the claim at the first position where we
freely chose a move — the forced opening is the corpus's, not ours.

**Finding:** loss (or tie) from a claimed-won position. **Resolution, in
order:** (1) ruleset mismatch — an engine rejection/illegal move; (2) our
move selector — any of our positions where the played move's value is below
the table's own extremum over children + pass; (3) table wrong — clean
rules, optimal play throughout, still lost: the bracket over-claims.

**Read of the table.** The informative row is the last-but-one: from every

## 5. Controls — both before the reading counts

**Null control (determinism of the harness).** A completed game's recorded
move list is replayed through a *fresh* engine instance (play only, no
genmove) and the harness must reproduce the identical final score with
every move accepted:

| engine | game replayed | recorded score | replay score | engine accepted all moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GNU Go | gnugo-o33-B | B+16 | B+16 | yes |
| Pachi | pachi-o33-B | B+16 | B+16 | yes |
| Fuego | fuego-o00-B | B+2 | B+2 | yes |

All three pass: the harness is deterministic (same input → same output).
Note the engines themselves are not: GNU Go's games varied 10/68 and
Pachi's 52/68 across two runs (their internal RNG); each run is a valid
sample, and the headline 0/N is stable across runs for all three engines.

**Seeded control (the checker catches a wrong move).** Inject a
deliberately wrong move for our side at one ply and show the checker flags
the resulting bracket escape:

- `--seedctl gnugo-o33-B 0 pass` — at the empty goban (root [1,16]
  claimed), force a pass. The checker flags ply 0 `optimal=false`
  (chosen value -1 vs extremum 1); the claim flips to White-favoured
  [-16,-1] at the next position; we recover only to B+1. Bracket escaped,
  flagged.
- `--seedctl gnugo-o02-B 2 pass` — root [16,16] claimed; injected pass at
  our first decision flips a forced 16-point win into a **W+9 loss**:
  the checker reports `finding=loss_from_claimed`,
  `resolution=selector` (the played move's value -16 is below the table's
  extremum 16), `outcome_inside_root=false`. The injection is caught at
  the injected ply and the cause attributed to the non-optimal move.

The seeded control proves the instrument's finding logic is sensitive: a
wrong move by our side is detected exactly where the brief requires.

**Score authority.** `--selfscore <game>` recomputes a recorded game's
score from its move list with no engine involved (pure `area_score` of the
final position); it matches the recorded score for every game checked —
the result is a pure function of the position, as the ruleset requires.

## 6. Working invocations — part of the deliverable

The operator knew only GNU Go's invocation; these were found and verified
by this task. All three are GTP engines reading commands on stdin. The
instrument's ruleset probes passed for all three with these invocations.

**GNU Go 3.8** (`/opt/homebrew/bin/gnugo`):

```
gnugo --mode gtp --boardsize 4 --chinese-rules --komi 0 --forbid-suicide --simple-ko --never-resign
```

`--simple-ko` is GNU Go's default and matches basic ko; `--never-resign`
is required because GNU Go's resign heuristic is broken on 4×4 (it resigns
even drawn positions); resignation is a politeness heuristic, not a
ruleset property, so forbidding it keeps the ruleset identical while the
engine plays to two passes.

**Pachi 12.90** (`/opt/homebrew/bin/pachi`):

```
pachi resign_threshold=0 threads=2 -t =1000
```

`-t =1000` = fixed 1000 Monte-Carlo simulations per move (the brief's
suggested per-move budget); `resign_threshold=0` disables resignation
(Pachi otherwise resigns most 4×4 positions); `threads=2` keeps the host
polite. Pachi's default ruleset is Chinese; its `final_score` works on an
empty board but returns `unclear groups` on contested 4×4 positions — not
used. Pachi lacks `all_legal` and `list_stones`.

**Fuego 1.1.SVN** (`/opt/homebrew/bin/fuego`):

```
fuego --quiet
```
then, over GTP:
```
boardsize 4
clear_board
komi 0
time_settings 0 1 1
uct_param_player resign_threshold 0
go_param_rules ko_rule simple
```

`go_param_rules ko_rule simple` is **mandatory**: Fuego's default is
positional superko (a different game; §2.2). `time_settings 0 1 1` caps
each move at 1 s (default 10 s byo-yomi made the corpus ~20 min; 1 s
makes it ~4 min); `uct_param_player resign_threshold 0` disables
resignation (Fuego otherwise resigns most 4×4 positions). With these
settings Fuego's `final_score` is numerically correct on the probe shapes
but its dead-stone judgment diverges from pure area on contested
positions — the game result always comes from our area score.

## 7. Caveats — what this does and does not claim

- **Fresh-start claims only (C1).** The bracket values are fresh-start
  values. A win in play does not certify the fresh-start value as a
  real-game value (C2 is falsified at 3×2, T13); and a *loss* from a
  claimed-won position under optimal fresh-start play would be a finding
  about the claim's playability, not a proof either way about C1. No such
  loss occurred, so the question did not arise.
- **Ko-sensitive rows are the untrusted region** (foreclosure): 14 of the
  107 claimed-won roots are ko-sensitive rows; all 14 were won. The
  claims held in play, but that is a play result, not Track A's
  regeneration.
- **Sample**: 68 games per engine, 204 total, from 34 openings. "0" is
  "none detected in 204 games", not a proof. The openings sample forced
  mid-game positions, many losing for one colour; they do not sample the
  full state space.
- **Engine RNG**: game move-sequences vary run to run (GNU Go 10/68,
  Pachi 52/68 across two runs; Fuego similar); the harness is
  deterministic (null control) and every run of every engine produced
  0 losses from claimed-won.
- **The capped game** (fuego-o32-W, basic-ko run) cycled legally to the
  400-ply cap; reported, not classified.
- **GNU Go is a weak 4×4 player**; its losses are opening-driven. That is
  the point: the test is asymmetric, and even a weak opponent cannot take
  a claimed-won position from us — and a strong one (Fuego) could not
  either.
