The mechanisms are unchanged from T366's analysis and are not re-argued here:

- **Frame A** — the old engine loses to itself: its own table contradicts
  itself (node values its children cannot reach, `fA-o21-B`; winning lines that
  collapse, `fA-o32-W`, `fA-o19-W`), consistent with the checkpoint being the
  known-untrustworthy writes-ON build.
- **Frame B** — the four additional losses (`fB-o02/o04/o06/o17`) come from
  basic-ko position repetition in the endgame: the new engine (White) plays
  position-repeating moves legal under basic ko but illegal under PSK, and the
  old engine's PSK rule and PSK-era table cannot answer — it passes out and
  loses 0–16. The kifu files mark these with a `NOTE: this move repeats a
  position the old engine's PSK rules forbid (cross-ruleset ply)`.

Each row's SGF is the committed `t366-<game>.sgf` in this directory (e.g.
`t366-fA-o32-W.sgf`), unchanged.

## 5. What this means — and what it does not

- **The old-vs-new 4×4 finding is stable.** Re-running the unchanged instrument
  15 days and 69 `src/` commits after T366, against the same pinned artifacts,
  reproduces every game, every divergence node, and every classification
  bit-for-bit. The engine changes in the interval are confined to GTP, buffer,
  and scoring-prose fixes and do not reach the oracle/solve/retro paths the
  instrument exercises — so a stable result is the expected outcome and is what
  was observed.
- **Where the old 4×4 engine loses** (unchanged from T366): (1) its own table
  contradicts itself — node values its children cannot reach, and lines that
  collapse from winning positions — consistent with the checkpoint being the
  known-untrustworthy writes-ON build; (2) in the new ruleset it cannot answer
  basic-ko position repetition. Rate: 3/66 (4.5%) in the shared-PSK frame,
  7/66 (10.6%) in the new-ruleset frame, at these openings.
- **No register claims.** The kifu neither refutes nor confirms a registered
  row; it is reproduction evidence for `L2 (proven 4×4 values)`.
- **What this does not claim:** no table value here is a real-game value —
  everything compared is the fresh-start slice (C1); real-game
  history-independence (C2) is falsified at 3×2 and is out of scope. The new
  engine's L/H values at ko-pending or pass states are reported as data, not
  used as the basis for a loss verdict.
